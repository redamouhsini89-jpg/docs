Most B2B SaaS products are sold to companies, not individuals. A single account might have dozens of users with wildly different activity levels — a power user who lives in your product every day alongside colleagues who have barely logged in. Your CS and sales teams need a single number that fairly represents the health of the entire account, not just its most or least active member. Sherlock scores at both the **user level** and the **account level**, giving your team a complete picture at every layer of your customer base.

## How Account Scores Are Calculated

Sherlock derives an account's score by **aggregating the scores of all users associated with that account**. The aggregation is not a simple average — it applies a **recency-weighted rollup** that accounts for how recently each user has been active:

- Users who have been active in the last 7 days carry the most weight in the rollup.
- Users active in the last 8–30 days carry moderate weight.
- Users with no activity in the last 30 days contribute minimally to the account score.

This approach ensures that an account stays healthy in Sherlock's eyes only when its users are *currently* engaged, not just because someone was highly active three months ago.

## Top-User Weighting

When an account has many users, a small number of power users could theoretically inflate the account score far above what is representative of the broader team's engagement. Sherlock prevents this with **top-user weighting**: the contribution of any single user to the account score is capped, so the score reflects meaningful engagement across the user base rather than the outlier behaviour of one champion.

This means an account with 10 moderately engaged users will typically outscore an account with 1 highly engaged user and 9 inactive ones — a more accurate signal of true account health.

## Account Score Range

Account scores follow the same **0–100 scale** as user scores and are normalised relative to all accounts in your workspace. A score of 0 means no associated user has had qualifying activity in the active time window. A score of 100 means this account is the most engaged in your workspace right now.

## User-to-Account Association

Sherlock builds the user-to-account relationship using Segment's **`group()` calls**. When your application calls `group()` with a `userId` and a `groupId`, Sherlock automatically associates that user with the corresponding account. No additional configuration is required on the Sherlock side — as long as your Segment instrumentation includes `group()` calls, account scoring works out of the box.

<Note>
  An account must have **at least one associated user** with a recorded score before Sherlock can assign it an account score. Accounts with no linked users appear in the Accounts tab but display a score of 0 until a user association is established via a Segment `group()` call.
</Note>

## Viewing Account Scores in the Dashboard

Navigate to the **Accounts** tab in the Sherlock sidebar to see every account in your workspace alongside its current engagement score.

From the Accounts tab you can:

- **Sort** accounts by score (highest or lowest first) to instantly surface your most and least engaged customers.
- **Filter** by score range, subscription plan, or account size to zero in on the segment that matters for a given workflow — for example, high-score accounts on a free plan who are ready for an upgrade conversation.
- **Click any account** to open the account detail view, which shows a score trend chart, all associated users and their individual scores, and a full event timeline for the account.

This view is the daily starting point for CS teams running health checks and for sales teams hunting for expansion opportunities among existing customers.
